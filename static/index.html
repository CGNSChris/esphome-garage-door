Garage Door Controller

Self-hosted ESPHome firmware — two endstops, a real state machine, and no dependency on the network to open your door. Source: CGNSChris/esphome-garage-door.

Install over USB

You don't need to pick a chip. Plug the board in and the installer identifies it over serial, then flashes the matching firmware. Supported boards:

BoardChipConfig
ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1-N16R8ESP32-S3garage-door.yaml
ESP32-C6-DevKitC-1-N8ESP32-C6garage-door-c6.yaml

Needs Chrome or Edge (Web Serial). If your board has two USB ports, use the UART one. If the installer says the chip isn't supported, it has detected something other than an S3 or C6 — check you have the right board before forcing anything.

Wiring is identical either way

The pin map is deliberately chosen from GPIOs that are safe on both chips, so one harness works with either board and swapping boards needs no rewiring.

Closed endstopGPIO18
Open endstopGPIO10
Relay triggerGPIO7
Status LEDGPIO2

A 10 kΩ pull-down on the relay GPIO is mandatory — see the wiring guide. Bench-test before connecting anything to the opener.

Updates

After the first flash the device updates itself over WiFi from manifest.json, which carries a build for each chip. A device only ever matches its own chipFamily, so an S3 cannot pull C6 firmware or vice versa. Checked every 6 hours; installed when you press Install in Home Assistant.
